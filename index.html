01 / Context

Situation

A two-decade transformation has created a large, profitable cloud business. The AI investment cycle raises the stakes.

Amazon set out its AWS vision in 2003 and launched its first storage and computing services, S3 and EC2, in 2006, selling outside customers the same infrastructure it had built to run its own retail business (Amazon 2023 Letter to Shareholders, Apr 2024). Twenty years later, AWS produced $128.7 billion in 2025 sales and $45.6 billion in operating income, about 57% of Amazon’s total operating income (Amazon 2025 Form 10-K, filed Feb 6, 2026). The transformation has now entered an AI phase: Amazon expects to invest about $200 billion in capital spending across the company in 2026 (Amazon Q4 2025 Earnings Release, Feb 5, 2026).
02 / Strategic findings

Three findings explain the trajectory

The analysis is organized around the strategic argument, rather than the sequence of frameworks.

FINDING 01

Demand is ahead of supply

AWS changed what Amazon is, and its revenue is recurring and pre-committed. AWS did not digitize retail; it created a new business with different customers and a different profit mechanism. That business earns high, rising margins on ongoing, on-demand usage: operating margin was 35.4% for 2025 and 39.4% in Q2 2026, when AWS earned $16.6 billion on $42.2 billion in sales, growing 37% (Amazon Q2 2026 Earnings Release, Jul 30, 2026). Demand is already locked in ahead of supply. AWS’s backlog reached $496 billion, growing at triple digits year over year (Amazon Q2 2026 Earnings Call, Jul 30, 2026). The open question for AWS is not whether customers will buy. It is whether Amazon can deliver.

FINDING 02

Physical capacity is the constraint

The binding constraint is physical capacity, not strategy, talent or demand. On strategy, data, process and people, AWS operates at the highest level of maturity. The limit is chips, power and buildings. Amazon’s CEO wrote that AWS still has “capacity constraints that yield unserved demand,” even after adding 3.9 gigawatts of power in 2025 (Amazon 2025 Letter to Shareholders, Apr 2026). On the Q2 2026 call he said AWS “will still not have enough capacity to meet all the demand we have in 2026,” and expects to double its 2025 power capacity by the end of 2027 (Amazon Q2 2026 Earnings Call, Jul 30, 2026). Every dollar of backlog depends on capacity that does not exist yet.

FINDING 03

AI extends the original playbook

AWS is repeating its original move with AI, which deepens the advantage. AWS is building its own AI chips and selling them to outside customers, just as it once sold the servers it built for itself. Its chips business and its AI business have each passed a $25 billion annual run rate, both growing at triple digits, and Anthropic and OpenAI have each made multi-year, multi-gigawatt commitments to AWS’s Trainium chips (Amazon Q2 2026 Earnings Release, Jul 30, 2026). AWS pairs those chips with Bedrock, a marketplace that offers outside AI models from several providers, which keeps AWS the place customers build regardless of which model wins. However, Amazon does not disclose the return it earns on AI capital spending. Its public metrics are mostly lagging: sales and operating income report results after the fact, and backlog shows demand but not return, so a slowdown in returns on the $200 billion would show up in margins only after the money is spent.

Causal synthesis

How these connect: Infrastructure built to a very high standard for Amazon’s own use could be sold as services, which created recurring, high-margin revenue. That revenue now funds an AI build-out large enough that capacity, not demand, sets the pace, and the return on that build-out is the one number leadership does not report.

03 / Judgment

Verdict

AWS is on a path to durable competitive advantage, not sophisticated digitalization: it changed Amazon’s revenue model, profit mechanism and competitive boundaries, and it is extending that position into AI chips and models. The single most important factor in the outcome is how efficiently Amazon converts capital spending into delivered capacity and booked revenue.

04 / Next 90 days

Recommendation

A specific governance action to connect capacity delivery, committed demand, and capital allocation.

Proposed executive action

The Amazon CFO and the AWS CEO should launch a quarterly capacity-to-revenue scorecard, reviewed by the board, that tracks for each new data-center tranche the gigawatts brought online, the share already covered by backlog commitments, and the months from power-on to first revenue, and should require that any 2027 capital spending beyond committed backlog be approved against it.

Rationale. Capacity is the binding constraint and the largest spending line has no reported return measure.

90-day success measure: The first scorecard is reviewed by the board and every 2027 spending request is tied to a specific share of the $496 billion backlog.
